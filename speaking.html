Speaking topics

More topics are in development, including a new session built around the A3 Framework, the method behind fixing the voice problem inside growing companies. Here’s what’s ready now.

Most people think following up means sending more messages. It doesn’t.

The real problem isn’t frequency. It’s that most people communicate in a way that pushes people away without ever realizing it. They disappear for months and reappear like nothing happened, they pitch before they’ve built any trust, and they run a process built for transactions instead of relationships. The result is that people forget you, ignore you, or lose respect for you.

This session takes a different approach. Drawing on the ancient Bushido code of the Samurai and real client stories, you’ll get a three part framework for building relationships that last for years. One of Ely’s clients came back ten years and three months after their first conversation, not to hire him again, but to refer his son’s business. That kind of loyalty doesn’t come from a clever pitch.

Whether you’re at a networking event, on a sales call, or just trying to stay in front of the people who matter, these principles work across every medium and every industry.

Who It’s For

Professionals who sell through relationships rather than transactions, and anyone whose prospects need to trust them before they buy.

Why Words Actually Matter

Why I do this

I started my first business in 1995. A web and graphic design agency, built from nothing into a five person office, almost entirely through in-person networking. I learned early that people don’t buy from strangers. They buy from people they trust, and trust takes time.

For years I marketed the way everyone told me to. Generic. Safe. Talking at people instead of talking to them, like a megaphone instead of a conversation. It worked, sort of. Not the way it should have.

So I changed it. I stopped trying to sound impressive and started sounding like myself. I stopped writing and talking to prospects like they were names on a list, and started treating them like actual people.

The results showed up fast, and not just for me. One client, Lori, had been in business four years, quietly building an email list the whole time with almost nothing to show for it. We changed how she talked to the people on that list. In her first three weeks under the new approach, more people replied thanking her for the emails and asking to get on her calendar than she’d ever had before.

It changed things for me too. People started coming up to me in rooms I’d never been in before, telling me they felt like they’d known me forever. Complete strangers, giving me a hug before we’d even been introduced. That is what happens when you stop broadcasting and start actually talking to people.

I wrote The Follow Up Code around seven virtues from the Bushido code of the Samurai, because the same principles that build trust in a relationship build trust in a business. Now I speak to rooms of professionals, and increasingly to whole companies, about that same idea. The words you use, and whether they sound like a real person or a marketing department, decide whether anyone remembers you at all.
